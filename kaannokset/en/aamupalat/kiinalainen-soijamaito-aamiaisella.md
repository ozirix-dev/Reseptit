# Chinese-style savoury warm soy milk (doujiang)

## Description

A small savoury hot soy drink seasoned with vinegar, sesame oil and spring onion.

## Ingredients

All measured ingredient quantities are in the structured list above.

## Instructions

1. Measure 200 ml unsweetened soy beverage, 15 ml unsweetened Lee Kum Kee Rice Vinegar (not sweetened sushi vinegar), 5 g sesame oil and 2 tablespoons chopped spring onion. Use only the listed optional salt. Contains soy and sesame; check labels.
2. Divide the rice vinegar between two heatproof serving bowls. Add half of the measured sesame oil to each, and a little of the listed salt if desired.
3. Heat all 200 ml soy beverage until steaming hot without a rolling boil. Pour into the bowls over the vinegar and let stand briefly. It may curdle slightly, depending on the soy beverage.
4. Scatter all the measured spring onions between both bowls and serve warm straight away. Do not add tofu, beans or other sauces.

## Notes

The soy beverage may not form distinct curds; the result is a small hot savoury soy soup. The labelled Lee Kum Kee vinegar has less than 0.5 g carbohydrate per 100 ml (under 0.04 g per serving here); its small contribution remains omitted from the estimated macros within the documented uncertainty bound. Not suitable for sweetened sushi vinegar. Contains soy and sesame; check labels.
